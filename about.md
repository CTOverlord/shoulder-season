<h1>The Shoulder Season</h1>
<p class="standfirst">An independent daily briefing on New Zealand's visitor economy.</p>
</div>

<div class="post-body measure" style="margin-top:44px">

<p>Every weekday we scan the announcements, data releases, openings, route changes and industry moves that shape tourism in Aotearoa, and boil them down to one five-minute read. No paywall, no fluff, no press-release padding. Just what happened, why it matters, and a link to the source if you want to go deeper.</p>

<p>The Shoulder Season is written for two audiences at once: the people who work in tourism, and the people who simply love following it. If you run a lodge, guide walks, plan itineraries, market a region, or just want to know how one of the country's biggest export earners is tracking, this is for you.</p>

<h2>Why "The Shoulder Season"?</h2>

<p>In tourism, the shoulder season is the quiet stretch between the peaks. It's when the industry catches its breath, takes stock and gets ready for what's next. That's the spirit of this briefing: the steady, unglamorous work of keeping up, done daily.</p>

<h2>The Diary</h2>

<p>Alongside the daily briefing we keep <a href="{{ '/diary/' | relative_url }}">a running diary</a> of everything dated we're tracking: conferences, company results, route launches, consultation deadlines, rule changes and events with visitor impact. It's updated each weekday morning.</p>

<h2>How we work</h2>

<p>Every item links to its original source. We summarise, we don't republish. When we quote a statistic, we name where it came from. Briefings are researched and drafted with the help of AI tools and edited before publishing. If we get something wrong, we correct it and say so.</p>

<p>The Shoulder Season is independently published. What we cover, and how we cover it, is decided by no one but us.</p>
