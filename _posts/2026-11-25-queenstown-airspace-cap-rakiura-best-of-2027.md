It's Wednesday, and the busiest piece of sky in the country is about to get a speed limit of its own. Five items.

## Airways caps Queenstown airspace at 38 movements an hour, and the scenic operators push back

Airways NZ will limit Queenstown airspace to 38 aircraft movements an hour for about six weeks over summer, and possibly two weeks in winter. The cap covers jets, fixed-wing aircraft and helicopters together, so the airlines, scenic flight operators and heli operators are all drawing on the same allowance. A movement is one arrival or one departure.

Operators say that is far below what the airspace has handled. Glenorchy Air chief executive James Stokes told the Herald movements had peaked at 80 an hour in the past, and the Aviation Industry Association says Queenstown ran safely at up to 55 an hour during one busy week last month. The association's chief executive, Simon Wallace, said members fear fewer scenic flights, more delays and damage to the town's reputation, and that travel wholesalers are already asking about reliability. Airways says the cap follows safety concerns an operator raised in 2023 and three years of talks about "uneven and at times excess capacity" in what it calls some of the most complex airspace in the country.

The cap lands just as the weak dollar is expected to send more international visitors south, and scenic flights are one of the most weather-sensitive products in the region: a morning of cloud already compresses a day's demand into a few hours. If 38 holds, the bottleneck on a clear afternoon is now the airspace rather than the aircraft.

## Rakiura and Ōneke make Condé Nast Traveller's 27 best places to go in 2027

Stewart Island/Rakiura and Ōneke are on Condé Nast Traveller's annual list of 27 places to go in 2027, a week after the BBC put Christchurch on its own list for the same year. The Herald report leans on the usual Rakiura facts: fewer than 400 permanent residents, 28 kilometres of road, about 85 percent of the island in national park, Dark Sky Sanctuary status, and an estimated 20,000 wild kiwi.

The practical question for a place this small is capacity, not awareness. The island's main ferry and tour operator, RealNZ, says it will run capped, small-group day packages this summer, so visitors can fit the island into a South Island trip without staying the night. Ōneke is visited with mana whenua guides through a joint venture with the Rakiura Māori Lands Trust. A global list brings in demand quickly. Beds, ferry seats and guided capacity on an island with no traffic lights grow much more slowly.

## Marlborough races El Niño to spray 650 hectares of wilding pines

Aerial spraying of 650 hectares of wilding conifers in the Molesworth and Branch-Leatham areas began on 20 November. The $1.6 million job is part of the $109 million national package in May's Budget, and the crews are working against the weather. Project director Marcus Girvan, of Boffa Miskell, told Local Democracy Reporting that hot, dry and windy El Niño conditions could arrive within weeks and force a pause, because the spray evaporates. He hopes to get at least half done first. The project is due to finish in February 2027.

Wildings matter to the visitor economy because they spread over the open tussock country that sells the back-country South Island, and Molesworth is the largest station in the country. A council biosecurity officer told a 12 November council meeting that the new money ends three years of underfunding. They also warned that without steady maintenance funding the trees reclaim the land and the money already spent is wasted. Ground crews face their own El Niño limits: fire risk could restrict chainsaws and other equipment that throws sparks.

## Travel agents say the weak dollar is sending Kiwis to Rarotonga and Vietnam rather than Europe

The outbound side of the 17-year low in the dollar, which has been covered here this week, is now showing in bookings. Flight Centre NZ general manager Heidi Walker told RNZ that the US, Europe and Hawaii have become trips people plan hard for, while enquiries are rising for Samoa, the Cook Islands, Vietnam and Thailand. House of Travel chief executive David Coombes said Australia and the Cook Islands top his 2027 forward bookings.

For domestic operators the interesting comment came from economist Benje Patterson. He expects some families looking at 2027 school-holiday trips to think twice about going overseas and holiday at home instead, though a recovering economy may keep the effect small. On the inbound side he called the weak dollar "like a Black Friday sale" for international visitors and expects better yields this summer for businesses that serve them. Coombes also said high fuel prices are holding fares up for now.

## Queenstown finds more speed signs the police cannot enforce, including on the Cardrona ski road

After police agreed to refund 113 speeding tickets issued on Gorge Road under a 60km/h sign the law had never caught up with, council documents show at least three more Queenstown Lakes locations have signs that do not match the legal limit. One is the 60km/h electronic variable limit at the base of the Cardrona Ski Field Road, which has not been legalised. The others are Church Road in Luggate and three new streets in Kawarau Heights. Gorge Road was one of 76 locations in a district-wide speed review, and NZTA told RNZ the council applied to formalise that limit only in recent weeks.

It is a small story about paperwork, but it lands in a district where a large share of drivers are visitors in rental cars on unfamiliar roads, and where the signs are the only rule anyone sees. The council says it will confirm when the Gorge Road limit is entered in the National Speed Limit Register.
