It's Wednesday, and two data releases tell the same story from different ends: travel inside New Zealand is getting more expensive, and the domestic traveller is the one feeling it. Four items.

## Domestic airfares up 33.5 percent on a year, the biggest rise since April 2023

Stats NZ's selected price indexes for October, released on Tuesday, show domestic airfares up 13.6 percent on September, after a 9.6 percent rise the month before. Over the year they are up 33.5 percent, which Stats NZ says is the largest annual increase since the year ended April 2023. Its spokesperson Jason Attewell noted that fares reflect pricing set up to 12 months ahead, so fuel is one influence among several.

Fuel is the obvious one. Petrol rose 11.9 percent in the month and is 38.0 percent dearer than a year ago; diesel is up 71.2 percent on the year. Stats NZ says petrol has risen 40.8 percent and diesel 73.6 percent since February. For a touring or transport operator, that is the cost line behind every quote for the summer.

The visitor-facing lines moved too. Domestic accommodation prices rose 6.0 percent in October and 5.4 percent on the year. International airfares, which cover New Zealanders flying out, fell 5.6 percent in the month but are still 13.1 percent up on the year. Restaurant meals and ready-to-eat food rose 3.4 percent annually, the biggest contributor to food price inflation.

**Source:** [Stats NZ, Selected price indexes: October 2026](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/selected-price-indexes-october-2026/); [Stats NZ, Petrol and diesel prices rise in October](https://www.stats.govt.nz/news/petrol-and-diesel-prices-rise-in-october/)

## Hotels lift revenue per room 16 percent, and most of it is price

Horwath HTL's New Zealand Hotel Performance Focus for October, built on Hotel Data New Zealand figures, puts national revenue per available room up 16.3 percent on last October, a sharp step up from 5.5 percent in September. Occupancy among participating hotels rose 3.9 points to 75.6 percent, while the average daily rate rose 10.3 percent to $241. Rate did more of the lifting than occupancy.

The growth came from overseas guests. Participating hotels reported international room nights up about 12.5 percent and domestic room nights up only 2.3 percent. Auckland carried the month: revenue per room up 27 percent, occupancy up 8 points and rate up 14 percent, with a busy NZICC conference calendar, a Bledisloe Cup test and concerts behind it. Auckland accounted for about 81 percent of the national rise in occupied room nights. Outside Auckland, room nights grew just 1.8 percent.

The South Island picture is rate rather than volume. Christchurch sold about 3 percent fewer room nights, as an 18 percent rise in international room nights was outweighed by an 18 percent fall in domestic ones, though it still had the highest occupancy of the key markets at 84.6 percent. Queenstown's growth was entirely rate-led, with domestic room nights down about 9 percent. Read alongside the airfare figures above, the New Zealander travelling at home is paying more to get there and staying fewer nights.

**Source:** [Horwath HTL, New Zealand Hotel Performance Focus October 2026 (PDF)](https://horwathhtl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/New-Zealand-Hotel-Performance-Focus-Oct-2026.pdf)

## Final bids are in for StraitNZ, owner of the Bluebridge ferries

The sale of StraitNZ, the Cook Strait freight and ferry company that runs Bluebridge, is nearing a close. Final bids were called on Wednesday 11 November, The Daily Cargo News reported on Monday, citing the Australian Financial Review. Toronto-based Northleaf Capital has lodged a binding bid, and Swiss investor Partners Group is believed to be the other final bidder. Seller Morgan Stanley Infrastructure Partners put the business up for sale in February with expectations of about NZ$1.2 billion, having paid a reported NZ$500 million in late 2021.

Bluebridge is one of only two ways to take a vehicle between the islands, so its ownership matters to every self-drive, campervan and touring itinerary that crosses the strait. Nothing in the reporting suggests a change to sailings. The longer question is competition: KiwiRail's Interislander is due to take delivery of two new ferries in 2029, and whoever buys StraitNZ will be pricing against them.

**Source:** [The Daily Cargo News](https://www.thedcn.com.au/news/last-bidders-left-for-straitnz); [NBR](https://www.nbr.co.nz/infrastructure/canadian-northleaf-capital-takes-pole-position-for-straitnz/)

## DOC defends Goat Island parking that could cost $60 a day this summer

Monday's briefing covered DOC's extension of paid parking to eight more sites from December. One detail is now drawing fire. At Goat Island Marine Reserve, north of Auckland, there is no daily cap in summer, so at $2.50 a half hour a 12-hour stay would cost $60. A $15 daily cap applies only from May to September.

Paul Sieberhagen, owner of Goat Island Dive and Snorkel, told the NZ Herald the charge could deter visitors and hurt local businesses, and said DOC had "made a mockery of the submissions process". DOC's director of heritage and visitors, David Butt, said consultation was done in good faith, that feedback shaped the final proposal, and that 12 hours is an exceptionally long stay. DOC received 162 submissions, 10 of them from local businesses. Concession holders can apply for an exemption; the dispute is about their customers, who arrive in their own cars.
