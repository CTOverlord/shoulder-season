It's Friday, and the week ends with a genuine demand signal, a firm opening date at Aoraki and water through Greymouth. Here's what's moving.

## Tourism Holdings lifts its profit call two months after cutting it

Tourism Holdings told the market on Thursday it now expects FY26 underlying net profit after tax from continuing operations of about $46 million, above the $40 million to $43 million range it guided to in May. Reported net debt at 30 June came in at $436 million, below the $460 million to $470 million the company had signalled. Audited results land on 25 August.

The reason matters more than the number. thl put the upgrade down to a surge in late bookings across all markets, stronger vehicle sales in New Zealand and a favourable interest outcome, with North American bookings well ahead of last year and United States bookings running more than 50% higher in recent weeks. Free independent travellers booking campervans late is about as direct a read on inbound demand as the listed market offers, and it is the same American market that spent the autumn hesitating over Middle East conflict. The company is doing this while fielding two non-binding takeover approaches, one from a BGH Capital and Trouchet family consortium at $3.10 a share and an unnamed industry bidder at $3.30 to $3.40, so the upgrade arrives in the middle of an argument about what the business is worth.

## The country's longest suspension bridge opens at Aoraki on Tuesday

The Department of Conservation has set a date for the new Hooker Valley crossing: open to the public from 3pm on Tuesday 28 July, after a private event and a blessing from mana whenua. At 189 metres it is New Zealand's longest suspension bridge and longest single-span footbridge, and it cost $4.85 million. It replaces a much smaller structure closed in April last year when heavy rain and storms eroded the riverbank.

The Hooker Valley track is one of the country's most walked day routes, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors a year, and DOC estimates the new bridge is worth around $167 million to the Mackenzie District over the next decade. That figure is a projection rather than a measurement, but the direction is not in doubt: fifteen months of a broken headline walk in a district whose entire visitor proposition is Aoraki has been a real cost to operators there. District operations manager Sally Jones paired the announcement with a warning worth passing to guests, that the track can be very icy and extremely cold and people need to be ready for sub-alpine conditions.

## Ōtautahi's convention year is worth $74 million

Te Pae Christchurch has published its year to June: 56 conventions generating close to $74 million for the local economy, more than $60 million of that flowing directly into Canterbury, and an estimated 827 jobs supported across the region. Another 133 events, exhibitions, banquets, live shows and meetings, brought total attendance past 125,000. Since opening in May 2022 the centre has hosted more than 865 events and 422,000 visitors.

Business events are the part of the visitor economy that behaves least like the rest of it. Delegates come in winter and shoulder months, book accommodation in blocks, spend more per head than leisure visitors and are locked in years ahead, which is exactly the counter-cyclical volume Christchurch needed. The venue's own year ahead illustrates the pull: TECNZ in August, AHICE Aotearoa the week after and Tourism Summit Aotearoa in November are all in the same building.

## The inbound trade puts distribution at the centre of its August conference

The Tourism Export Council has released the programme for its annual conference, "Tourism Unlocked, New Opportunities", running 5 and 6 August at Te Pae Christchurch. Sessions cover offshore distribution, commercial contracting, artificial intelligence, succession planning and workforce, with Infometrics chief executive Brad Olsen, Wētā Workshop co-founder Sir Richard Taylor and Tourism Holdings chief executive Grant Webster among the speakers, alongside US-based inbound specialist Frank Krieger and Sydney representation executive Jacqui Walshe. Chief executive Lynda Keene says the focus is global trends, innovation and growth.

The line-up is the message. Two of the six named speakers work the offshore end of the channel, which reflects where the inbound sector's anxiety actually sits: not whether demand exists, but whether New Zealand product stays visible and contracted in overseas distribution as booking behaviour shifts. Webster speaking a fortnight before his own audited results, with two bidders circling, will be worth the room on its own.

## A cloudburst floods Greymouth and shuts State Highway 7

Grey District was hit overnight by what West Coast Emergency Management called a cloudburst-style rain event, flooding about half a dozen homes across Greymouth, Cobden, Brunner and Rūnanga and bringing down a slip on Cobden road. State Highway 7 closed between Greymouth and Stillwater, with NZTA advising drivers to avoid the area or delay travel. Mayor Tania Gibson said crews had been out since 2am and there was a reasonably significant clean-up ahead.

For anyone routing guests across the island this is a live problem rather than a historical one, with more heavy rain forecast through the day and the position on the highway still moving. SH7 is one of only three sealed alpine crossings, so a closure there pushes traffic onto Arthur's Pass or Haast in the middle of a winter week. Check NZTA before committing to a West Coast leg today.
