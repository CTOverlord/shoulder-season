It's Monday, the school holidays are done and the industry's attention is on Queenstown for two days. Here's what's moving.

## WiT opens in Queenstown with a billion-dollar side agenda

Web in Travel, Asia Pacific's travel-tech conference, runs its first Australasian edition in Queenstown today and tomorrow under the theme "The Next 20". The speaker list is unusually senior for a debut: Air New Zealand chief executive Nikhil Ravishankar, Agoda's Omri Morgenshtern, SiteMinder's Sankar Narayan and Webjet's Katrina Barry, with AI disruption, digital identity and loyalty on the agenda.

The local angle is the more interesting one. Technology Queenstown, the trust hosting the event with foundation members including Queenstown Airport, Destination Queenstown, One NZ and the University of Otago, has a stated goal of building NZ$1 billion of annual tech output in the Queenstown Lakes District over 20 years, lifting tech from under 2% of the district economy to more than 15%. Tourism is currently over 60% of it. The pitch is to export the booking, logistics and payments know-how the town already has rather than keep importing software, which is a rare example of a tourism-dependent region trying to diversify using tourism itself as the raw material.

## Queenstown's roadworks are now a visitor-experience problem

Robins Rd, the link between Gorge Rd and the Queenstown CBD, has been closed since January. A partial reopening promised for April has slipped to August and the full reopening from June to September, blamed on machinery trouble and hard ground while a new wastewater pipe goes in. Late-afternoon queues on Gorge Rd now run back past the Industrial Place turn-off, and half of Hallenstein St closes for three weeks from next week for water connection work.

NZSki chief executive Paul Anderson has put the commercial case bluntly: the delays are degrading the visitor experience, and skiers who compare the drive with Perisher or Thredbo may not come back. Retailers report the damage is already measurable, with one central store down about 10% on sales since the works began and the 4pm to 6pm winter peak now its quietest window. Infrastructure delivery rarely makes a destination's marketing plan, but it is showing up in the numbers this season.

## A 30-year zipline bid on conservation land is open for submissions

RCT Group, the company behind Rotorua Canopy Tours and Queenstown's Million Dollar Cruise, has applied to the Department of Conservation for a 30-year lease and licence to run a guided zipline operation between Queenstown and Glenorchy, using the Twelve Mile Delta Recreation Reserve and Mt Crichton Scenic Reserve. The proposal is eight platforms, each under 25sqm, and four ziplines from 30m to 300m, linked by new and upgraded track, with a maximum of 13 customers and two guides per trip.

Capacity is the number to watch: up to 20 trips a day in summer and 11 in winter, a theoretical ceiling of 260 customers daily. The company argues the long term is justified by heavy upfront capital and a five to ten year run to break-even, which is a fair description of the economics but also the reason long concessions attract scrutiny. The application is publicly notified and submissions close on 7 August.

## Group and tour escort visas move online in August, not July

Immigration New Zealand has pushed back the move of Group Visitor, Tour Escort Visitor and ADS free and independent traveller visa applications onto its enhanced Immigration Online platform. The new date is 24 August, with the agency citing the need for more final testing before switching over. Applications submitted in the current system in the meantime will not be disrupted.

For inbound operators and agents this is a genuine workflow change rather than a cosmetic one. The new system lets an applicant share a Group Visitor Visa application with other group members and with tour agency staff, which removes a chunk of the document-chasing that currently sits with the ground handler. Worth confirming with your Asian wholesale partners which system their applications will land in over the changeover week.

**Source:** [Immigration New Zealand](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/about-us/news-centre/group-and-tour-escort-visitor-visa-applications-moving-to-enhanced-immigration-online/)

## Wellington's hospitality is leaning on August to carry it

Wellington On a Plate runs 1 to 31 August with more than 200 ticketed and free events, and organisers say more than half of all tickets are already sold with around 13 events sold out. Festival director Beth Brash says regional participation is the biggest it has been, with growth in Kāpiti and the Hutt Valley.

The context is a hospitality sector still absorbing higher food, wage and energy costs, with closures running above pre-pandemic levels. Last year the festival and Beervana together were credited with a $10 million lift, turning August from the capital's quietest hospitality month into its second busiest after Christmas. Brash's read is that the affordable end is holding up better than the full evening spend, which is the same pattern operators are seeing in day-trip and add-on bookings around the country.
