It's Monday, and a quiet post-election weekend still produced one decision that lands on a lot of itineraries. Four items.

## DOC extends paid parking to eight more sites from December

The Department of Conservation will start charging for parking at eight more of its busiest sites when the summer season starts in December: Tasman Glacier, the Kepler Track control gates, Kura Tāwhiti/Castle Hill, Blue Pools, Routeburn Divide, Deepwater Basin at Piopiotahi Milford Sound, Devils Punchbowl and Te Hāwere-a-Maki/Goat Island marine reserve. It follows this year's pilot at Punakaiki, Franz Josef Glacier and White Horse Hill.

At most sites the first 10 minutes are free for drop-offs, then it is $2.50 a half hour, capped at $20 a day. Goat Island has no daily cap in the high season and a $15 cap from May to September, and DOC's own page lists Deepwater Basin prices as still to be announced. Payment is by card at machines on site, with no cash and no pre-booking, and overstaying draws a $75 breach notice. Locals inside set boundaries can buy a $10 annual permit covering two vehicles. DOC expects about $3 million across all sites in the first year, which David Butt, its director of heritage and visitors, says will go back into conservation regionally and nationally.

For the trade, the detail that matters is the exemption. Commercial operators with a valid DOC concession can apply to be exempt, as can school, volunteer and community groups and DOC contractors, with applications opening in December. An operator who drops guests at Blue Pools or the Routeburn under a concession should have that exemption sorted before the first busy weekend. A free-independent traveller doing a West Coast and Southern Lakes loop now meets a meter at several stops, and the ones staying all day at Tasman Glacier or the Kepler pay $20 for each.

## Air New Zealand will fly Hamilton to Queenstown from June

Air New Zealand announced at the weekend that it will start Hamilton-Queenstown flights in June 2027, three return flights a week on Mondays, Thursdays and Saturdays, year-round, using 171-seat A320s. That is about 53,300 seats a year across both directions. Today, Waikato travellers heading to the Southern Lakes have to go via Auckland, Wellington or Christchurch.

Hamilton Airport handled a record 530,000 passengers last year and already runs A320s to Christchurch, so the airport needs no new investment for the route. Its chief executive, Mark Morgan, pitched it in both directions, linking Queenstown visitors with Hamilton Gardens, Hobbiton, Waitomo, Rotorua and Taupō. The June start puts the first flights at the beginning of the ski season. For a Southern Lakes operator, that is a new pool of winter guests from the central North Island. For the Waikato, it puts the region one flight away from where many inbound visitors already are. Air New Zealand chief executive Nikhil Ravishankar called it an example of the airline making "deliberate choices about where we grow". That fits Friday's schedule news here, which added frequency on existing international routes and no new ones.

## Christchurch reopens its grant for researchers who can bring conferences home

ChristchurchNZ Business Events, backed by Tourism New Zealand Business Events, has opened a second round of its Emerging Global Leaders Grant. The grant pays early and mid-career researchers and professionals to go to international conferences, take committee or workshop roles, or do governance training, so that they are in the room when an association picks its next host city. Applicants need to be active in an international association whose conference usually draws 500 to 2,000 delegates.

The first round backed four people from the University of Canterbury and the University of Otago. So far that has won one international conference for Christchurch in 2028, with two more bids under way in fields including data science, aerospace, product design and health. Te Pae, which opened in 2022, has hosted more than 300 conferences, with an estimated economic impact for New Zealand of almost $74 million. Bidding for association conferences takes years and depends on local people, and a small grant aimed at the right academics is one of the cheaper ways a convention city can get in early. Applications close on 31 December, and successful applicants hear by 29 January.

## Orange rain warnings on the West Coast run into Monday afternoon

MetService's orange heavy rain warnings for Westland, the headwaters of the Canterbury lakes and rivers around and south of Arthur's Pass, and the Grey and Buller ranges run until Monday afternoon. It forecast up to 400mm in the Westland ranges over two days, with peaks of 40mm an hour about the ranges and the main divide on Monday morning, and a strong wind watch for Wellington and Wairarapa from midday to 8pm. NZTA has warned of slips, surface flooding and strong crosswinds, and told campervan and other high-sided vehicle drivers to take particular care in exposed places.

When this briefing went out on Monday morning, NZTA's live map showed no weather closures on South Island state highways. Rivers on the Coast rise quickly, though, and anyone with guests crossing Haast, Arthur's Pass or Lewis Pass today should check the [Journey Planner](https://www.journeys.nzta.govt.nz/) again before leaving.
