It's Tuesday, and the biggest proposed ski expansion in the country has stopped to argue with its neighbours. Five items.

## NZSki pauses its fast-track bid to take The Remarkables into Doolans Basin

An expert fast-track panel has agreed to suspend NZSki's application to expand The Remarkables into neighbouring Doolans Basin for up to five months, according to a minute published on Friday and reported by RNZ. The plan is for what would be the country's biggest gondola, opening 262 hectares of south-facing terrain and lifting the resort's capacity to 6,000 visitors at a time, close to double today's. The pause gives NZSki, the councils, the Department of Conservation and Kā Rūnaka time to work through disputes that are both legal and technical.

The objections are substantial. DOC called the basin ecologically unique and of exceptionally high importance, said the earthworks alone would do irreversible damage to rare landforms, and disputed NZSki's plan to turn a tarn into a snow-making reservoir, which DOC regards as a high-value wetland and NZSki as a gravel depression. Otago Regional Council wants more on freshwater effects. Kā Rūnaka listed 12 concerns, including possible breaches of the Ngāi Tahu Treaty settlement. Queenstown Mountain Guides urged the panel to decline outright, arguing there are few comparable beginner and intermediate touring spots near town that need neither a long walk-in nor a helicopter. NZSki chief executive Paul Anderson points out that the company wants 262 hectares of a 33,000-hectare conservation area, and says the gondola would improve backcountry access rather than end it.

The timetable is what operators in the Wakatipu should note. NZSki owes the panel a progress report by 29 January and has until 20 April 2027 to ask for processing to resume; Anderson says the company is aiming for mid-February. Fast-track was meant to shorten exactly this kind of fight. The minute shows it can still stall when the agency holding the land and the treaty partner both say no.

## Partners Group agrees to buy StraitNZ, owner of the Bluebridge ferries

Last Wednesday's briefing reported that final bids were in for StraitNZ. The buyer is now named: Swiss investment firm Partners Group has agreed to buy the company from Morgan Stanley Infrastructure Partners, which bought it in 2022. StraitNZ runs two ships on daily Cook Strait freight and passenger sailings. Partners Group says it will work with management on a plan to improve the customer offering and grow revenue, and the deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to Overseas Investment Office approval.

For anyone selling a self-drive or touring itinerary that crosses the strait, the useful part is what has not changed: no word of any change to sailings, and a sale that will not complete before next winter. "Grow revenue" from a new infrastructure owner usually means price or capacity. It is worth watching which one arrives first, on a crossing with only two vehicle ferry operators.

## The dollar slides to a 17-year low just as households start spending again

The New Zealand dollar traded around US54.6c over the weekend, its lowest since the 2009 financial crisis, BNZ senior markets strategist Jason Wong told RNZ. Until a few weeks ago the driver was the Reserve Bank's low interest rates; now it is a global sell-off on share markets and nerves about the AI boom, and he says more weakness is possible if equities keep falling. That pushes yesterday's ASB view, a kiwi below US60c for quarters to come, a little further in the same direction.

On the same day Stats NZ reported retail sales volumes up 2.2 percent in the September quarter, the strongest quarterly rise in nearly five years, with 11 of 15 industries up and South Island sales 2 percent higher at $8.4 billion. Westpac warned that much of it was fuel, which fell in price during the quarter and has since risen again, though spending was firmer even without fuel. For domestic tourism this is the more useful number: a weak dollar makes overseas holidays dearer for New Zealanders, and the retail figures suggest some of them now have the money to spend on a trip at home.

## A West Coast swimming valley is under council investigation after heavy earthworks

West Coast Regional Council is investigating works by Birchfield Mining at Ten Mile Creek, north of Rapahoe near Greymouth, a valley locals value for its swimming holes, walking track and scenery, Local Democracy Reporting says. Residents have reported extensive excavation, vegetation clearance and riverbank work, and want to know whether gold recovery is involved. Compliance staff visited, and the council has asked for more information to see whether permitted-activity rules were breached. A separate consent application, for up to 5,000 cubic metres of gravel and rock a year from a 930-metre stretch, describes the effects as no more than minor. Regional councillor Allan Birchfield says no mining is happening and that the work is reopening a private road, removing old equipment and screening road gravel.

Small, free natural sites like this one do a lot of quiet work for the Coast's visitor offer, because they are what a self-drive traveller stops for between the headline attractions. The council's findings will show how much protection those sites actually have under permitted-activity rules.

**Source:** [RNZ (Local Democracy Reporting)](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/regional/2749782/severe-works-may-be-needed-in-much-loved-west-coast-valley)

## Butcher's Pool near Reporoa gets $145,000 and a changing room before Christmas

Rotorua Lakes Council has started its first significant upgrade in 25 years at Butcher's Pool, a free thermal swimming spot about 2km south of Reporoa that was gifted to the community in 1927. The pool has suffered vandalism and has a poor reputation online, where one reviewer likened it to a swamp. The work covers new steps and handrails, paths and seating, concrete repairs, changes to the water outlet, a small changing room and refreshed toilets. About $100,000 comes from the council and $45,000 from the Rotorua Rural Community Board, and the council says it will be finished before Christmas.

Board member Colin Guyton said the pool has increasingly become a destination for visitors, and that while it is free to use, its upkeep is not free to ratepayers. That is the free-attraction problem in miniature, the same one DOC answered with paid parking this month: a small rural rating base maintaining a site that visitors increasingly find through social media. Rotorua's answer here is a one-off grant and good manners rather than a charge.
