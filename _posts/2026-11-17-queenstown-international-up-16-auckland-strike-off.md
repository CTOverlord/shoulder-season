It's Tuesday, and the October airport numbers say more about this summer than anything out of the election count. Six items.

## October traffic: Queenstown international up 16 percent, Auckland flat

Auckland Airport's October traffic update, filed to the NZX on Monday, shows total passenger movements through Auckland unchanged on last October at 1.62 million. The international side grew: 903,182 international movements including transit, up 3 percent, with seats up only 2 percent, so planes were fuller, at an 85 percent average load factor. Among the top five nationalities, Chinese nationals rose 15 percent, Australians 11 percent and Americans 5 percent, while UK nationals fell 1 percent. Short-haul routes grew 6 percent. Long-haul movements slipped 1 percent on 4 percent fewer seats.

Domestic flying is still going the other way. Domestic movements at Auckland fell 3 percent on 5 percent fewer seats, which the airport puts down to airline capacity reductions. Trunk routes held level, but regional movements fell 9 percent on 8 percent fewer seats. A regional operator relying on a connection through Auckland is working with a smaller and fuller network than a year ago.

Queenstown, reported in the same filing, had a different month: total movements up 11 percent to 239,070, with international up 16 percent to 91,252 and domestic up 8 percent. That is roughly 12,600 more international movements than last October (this briefing's arithmetic from the filing). Over the past 12 months Queenstown has handled 1.14 million international movements, 17 percent more than the 12 months before.

**Source:** [Auckland International Airport, October 2026 monthly traffic update (NZX)](https://www.nzx.com/announcements/483685)

## Auckland Airport's emergency services strikes are off, for now

E tū withdrew its strike notice for Auckland Airport's emergency services team on Friday afternoon, so the two 24-hour strikes planned for Tuesday 24 and Friday 27 November will not go ahead. This briefing reported the notice last Wednesday, along with the airport's warning that flights could be cancelled or diverted.

The withdrawal is not a settlement. The union says talks produced an improved offer, which members will consider and vote on at meetings this week. Assistant national secretary Mat Danaher said the union was "pleased with the progress that has been made this week". Operators who built contingency plans around those two days can stand them down, but should keep an eye on the vote. The separate Fire and Emergency New Zealand strike on 27 November, which this briefing covered on Friday, is a different dispute and is unaffected.

**Source:** [E tū](https://etu.nz/e-tu-airport-fire-and-rescue-workers-withdraw-strike-notice-flights-to-operate-as-normal/); [CAPA](https://centreforaviation.com/news/e-t-withdraws-strike-notice-for-airport-emergency-services-team-atauckland-international-airport-1379878)

## Services activity hits a three-and-a-half-year high, led by accommodation and hospitality

The BNZ-BusinessNZ Performance of Services Index rose 0.8 points to 51.7 in October, its fifth month in a row above the 50 mark that separates expansion from contraction and its best reading in more than three and a half years. New orders, at 53.0, were the strongest part of it. Employment, at 49.9, was the only part still contracting.

BNZ senior economist Doug Steel said accommodation, cafes and restaurants were among the strongest sectors and tied that to international tourism. Respondents named summer, Christmas bookings and returning visitors as positives. Domestically focused sectors were weaker, with cultural, recreational and personal services falling sharply and retail subdued. That matches Friday's card spending data, where hospitality spending fell. Read together, the hospitality businesses doing well this spring are mostly the ones serving international visitors. Every region is growing, the South still ahead.

**Source:** [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/business/2628587/services-activity-hits-3-point-5-year-high)

## Air New Zealand goes into the mobile business with One NZ

Air New Zealand Mobile went on sale last Thursday. It is run with One NZ and has two products. One is a travel eSIM with data in more than 140 destinations, which can be bought for a whole trip including transit stops and works whatever network the customer is on. The other is a prepaid monthly plan with calls, texts and data for use in New Zealand. Pricing is on the new service's own site, not in the release.

The visitor-economy angle is the eSIM. International visitors can buy one before they arrive, which puts the national carrier and its partner in competition with the airport SIM stand for the inbound visitor's connectivity spend. It is also one more way for the airline to hold on to a customer between flights. Jeremy O'Brien, Air New Zealand's chief customer and digital officer, said connectivity is something people "need every day", not just when they fly.

**Source:** [Air New Zealand](https://www.airnewzealandnewsroom.com/press-release-2026-air-new-zealand-and-one-nz-team-up-to-bring-new-mobile-service-to-customers-at-home-and-abroad)

## Nelson hands its summer visitor information service to an arts agency

Nelson City Council has chosen Hiwa, the city's arts development agency, to run a temporary visitor information pop-up at Millers Acre from 1 December to March 2027. It will be open six days a week to start, then seven days from 18 December to 8 February, and the council will review the hours after Waitangi Day. It will use Hiwa's existing staff and gallery space, add QR codes and volunteer ambassadors in the city centre at busy times, and sell local art, crafts and food.

Mayor Nick Smith framed it as support for the recovery of the city's tourism sector. The model is the interesting part: rather than paying for a stand-alone office, Nelson is sharing an existing building and staff with an arts agency for the months that matter. The council will get monthly reports on visitor numbers, enquiries and referrals to local operators. Local operators should make sure the Hiwa team knows them before 1 December.

**Source:** [Nelson City Council via Scoop](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/AK2611/S00474/hiwa-to-bring-fresh-approach-to-nelsons-summer-visitor-information-pop-up.htm)

## Air Niugini lands in Auckland today

Air Niugini's first non-stop flight from Port Moresby touches down in Auckland today. It is the airline's first scheduled service to New Zealand in 26 years, following the international airline licence it was granted on 9 November. Auckland Airport said in July that the route would run three times a week on Boeing 737-800s. It is the only non-stop link between the two countries, a small but real addition to the Pacific network into Auckland.
