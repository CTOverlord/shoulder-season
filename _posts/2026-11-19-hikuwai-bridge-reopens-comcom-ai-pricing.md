It's Thursday, and the most useful news of the week for the East Cape is a bridge. Four items.

## The Hikuwai Bridge on State Highway 35 reopens, nearly four years after Cyclone Gabrielle

Both lanes of the new Hikuwai Bridge, on State Highway 35 between Tolaga Bay and Tokomaru Bay, opened on Wednesday. The old bridge was washed away in February 2023. Since then traffic has used a detour road through land owned by the Pourau Incorporation, then a one-lane Bailey bridge. The crossing is the link between Gisborne and Tolaga Bay to the south and Tokomaru Bay, Ruatōria and the East Cape to the north, which makes it the spine of the Pacific Coast Highway loop.

The replacement cost $50 million and is designed to sit under water in a future flood and take the load of debris piling against it. Transport Rebuild East Coast project manager Richard Bayley told RNZ the piles on one side go 50 metres down to reach hard rock. There is one more round of disruption: from Thursday the bridge goes back to one-way traffic while the Bailey bridge is lifted out, and Bayley expects the site to be fully open "by Christmas".

For visitor businesses on the coast this is a summer story. Pita Cunningham, who owns Cafe 35 in Tokomaru Bay, said he lost all trade for two months after the cyclone and expects a good summer, noting that travellers hesitate over Bailey bridges. Both he and Pourau Incorporation chair Philip Hope said the rest of SH35 still needs work, and Hope pointed to many other bridges on the route that need replacing.

## The Commerce Commission puts AI chatbots, fake reviews and shared pricing software on notice

The Commerce Commission published guidance on Wednesday on how the Fair Trading Act and the Commerce Act apply when businesses use artificial intelligence. It is guidance, not new law, and the message from deputy chair Anne Callinan is that nothing has changed: whether a decision is made by a person, an algorithm or an AI system, "the business remains accountable for the outcome".

Several parts land squarely on tourism and hospitality. A business can be liable if its AI-written advertising, website copy or chatbot answers mislead a customer, so a booking bot that gets a cancellation policy or an inclusion wrong is the operator's problem, not the software's. AI-generated reviews, ratings or testimonials that do not reflect genuine customer experiences may breach the Act. Personalised pricing has to be clear and accurate, and the Commission says some businesses should consider disclosing their use of AI to avoid creating a misleading impression.

The competition section matters most to accommodation, where revenue management software is common. The Commission warns that a shared pricing platform or software provider can raise concerns if it lets competitors exchange sensitive information or builds recommendations from competitors' confidential data, and says using an independent technology provider does not automatically protect a business. It suggests checking what data a pricing tool uses and keeping people in charge of its output. The Commission and the AI Forum are running a free webinar on the guidance on 24 November at 12pm.

## Starlux confirms its Auckland service from 23 March

Last Thursday this briefing reported Starlux's Taipei-Sydney-Auckland route as filed but not confirmed. The Taiwanese airline has now announced it, with the first flight on 23 March 2027, four return services a week on the Airbus A330-900 and daily flying from 1 May.

On the published schedule the flight from Sydney lands in Auckland at 4.10pm and the return leaves at 6.35pm, reaching Taipei at 4.05am. Starlux says it will use its partnership with Qantas to reach other destinations in the region. It is the airline's first route into Oceania, and it adds to Air New Zealand's own Taiwan growth, reported here on 13 November.

**Source:** [Focus Taiwan (CNA)](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202611170009)

## Tauranga launches its first place brand, two years and nearly $1.28 million in the making

TaurangaNZ, a brand for Tauranga and the Western Bay of Plenty, was launched on Wednesday. Tourism Bay of Plenty led the project with the two councils, mana whenua, economic development agency Priority One and the University of Waikato. Tauranga City Council put in $1.01 million and Western Bay of Plenty District Council $245,000, with partner agencies making up the rest of a budget of nearly $1.28 million. Auckland agency Guardians designed it, and more than 2,000 residents took part in the engagement and design phase.

The brand is pitched at people who might live, work, study, invest or holiday in the region, not at visitors alone, which is the usual shape of a place brand and the usual test of one. Priority One chief executive Dave Courtney said its job was to "drive our region's revenue line", and that success depends on councils, businesses and tourism operators telling a common story. The package includes a website, video, social channels and merchandise sold through the new i-SITE in the city library.
